Evaluation formula

The final grade has three components. Each component has a grade from 0% to 100%. There is no final exam.

  • 25% Submitted Implementations. Weekly problem sets.
  • 50% Participation in Competitive Events. Three on-site contests.
    • 30% 1st Contest
    • 30% 2nd Contest
    • 40% 3rd Contest
  • 25% Presentations, Discussion of Solutions and Problem Creation
    • 50% Problem Creation
    • 50% Code Presentation
  • Bonus points

(SI) Submitted Implementations [25% of your grade]

  • We will have 10 evaluation problem sets, each one amounting to 10% of the total grade.
  • Each problem set will have a deadline associated with it. The typical deadline will be two weeks after the problems are made public.
  • Each problem set has 5 problems. The first problem solved is worth 40%, the second one 40% and the third one 20%. This means that:
    • Solving one problem is worth 40%
    • Solving two problems is worth 80%
    • Solving at least three problems is worth 100%
  • For each day after the delivery date you will have a 4% penalty on the respective problem. For example:
    • 1 problem before the deadline and 1 problem three days after the deadline will give you 68% (40% for the first problem and 28% = 40% − 3×4% for the second problem)
    • 2 problems before the deadline and 1 problem ten days after the deadline will give you 80% (80% for the first two problems and 0% for the last, since the penalty of 10×4% is more than the 20% that the last problem would give you).

Failure to participate in at least two of these events will result in a RFC evaluation (lack of an evaluation component).

You can participate in extra contests (e.g. Codeforces or AtCoder Rounds) to compensate for any contest that did not go well for you (extra participations will be evaluated on a case by case basis).

(PDC) Presentations, Discussion of Solutions and Problem Creation [25% of your grade]

(PC) Problem Creation 50%

Each student should create a new problem in the style of a competitive programming event, together with all associated material (problem statement, solution and written editorial, inputs/outputs and generator).

Deadline: January (day to be defined).

(CQ) Code Presentation 50%

Quality of submitted code (including comments) and presentation (you will discuss with the professor the code you submitted).

Deadline: January (day to be defined).

Bonus points

You can obtain a bonus of up to +10% on your grade (e.g. to compensate some of the less strong evaluation items you had) with aspects such as:

  • Extra problems made (above the minimum for having 100% of the SI component)
  • Extra contests where the students participated
  • Participation in classes and on Discord
  • Any other relevant activities (e.g. writing articles about algorithms and data structures)
